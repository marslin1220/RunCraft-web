BEFORE THE RUN

Know what today is for.

See the scheduled session and its purpose alongside your recent running. Training pace guidance gives each type of work its own meaning.

  • Your scheduled work, rest days and weekly running at a glance
  • VDOT-based guidance for Easy, Marathon, Threshold, Interval and Repetition paces
  • A recent performance informs pace guidance; race-time predictions remain estimates
↗

Context, not a verdict.

Available sleep, HRV and resting-heart-rate observations add context. Dates and missing data matter: a reading does not confirm recovery or automatically prescribe a different workout.

DURING THE RUN

Take the session to your wrist.

Follow structured workouts on iPhone or Apple Watch, with the current step, pace and progress in view. Spend less time checking what comes next.

  • Follow work and recovery intervals
  • View pace and heart rate while recording
  • Use iPhone guidance when you do not have an Apple Watch

AFTER THE RUN

Keep the run. Choose the next step.

Review recorded distance, splits, pace and heart rate with available effort and weather context. Finishing a course opens a new decision, not an automatic leap in training.

  • Keep completed runs and course history
  • Review relevant saved background when planning again
  • Choose a new direction explicitly; breaks and recovery still need their own review

Before you lace up.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Guided workouts can run directly on iPhone. A paired Apple Watch adds on-wrist guidance and controls. Data availability depends on your devices and permissions.

Do I need a race or a VDOT value?

Not for every path. You can start with run-walk training or a free run. Some courses need relevant experience, pace guidance and enough preparation time.

Does saving a race create or replace my course?

No. Save the goal first, then arrange training separately. Existing sessions and history are retained; the course has its own review and preparation requirements.

Will poor sleep automatically change my workout?

No. Recovery observations provide context and caution. They do not automatically establish a replacement workout or certify that you are ready to train.

What happens after a course or a break?

Review the next direction and relevant saved background. A new course needs its own review; old records do not automatically confirm current preparation or recovery.

What happens to my data?

RunCraft does not require a RunCraft account or use advertising or third-party analytics. Data is processed on your devices; selected records can sync through your private iCloud database. Optional support exports are shared only when you choose.
