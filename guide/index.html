Guides

Using RunCraft

Set a goal, choose a course and follow your workouts, with your running history there when you plan again.

01

Choose how you want to run

Start a regular running programme, prepare for a race or go for a free run. To save a race goal, enter its date and exact distance. The name is optional. You can arrange training separately when you are ready.

02

Check your experience and available time

Review relevant runs and saved answers, including when they were recorded. A recent taper or recovery period may not reflect your usual running. Update anything that has changed, then answer the questions this course needs about your experience, days and time. Missing records do not mean you are a beginner.

03

Check whether the course fits

Review the sessions, dates and preparation requirements before choosing a course. Signing up for a race does not mean you are prepared for every workout it might involve. If the supported courses do not fit your distance, timeline or preparation, read the explanation in the app before choosing another direction.

04

Understanding your training paces

RunCraft uses VDOT to turn a performance value into pace guidance. Easy, Marathon, Threshold, Interval and Repetition work have different purposes. Predicted race times are estimates, and a VDOT value cannot show whether you have experience with every type of workout.

05

Reading sleep and heart-rate information

Sleep, HRV and resting heart rate need enough recorded history for comparison. Gaps or readings from several days ago cannot tell you how recovered you are today. Recent data alone cannot settle whether you are ready to train, either. Use it as a reason to check how you feel. RunCraft will not automatically replace a workout from these readings or provide a medical diagnosis.

06

Following and reviewing a workout

Use iPhone or a paired Apple Watch to follow the current interval, target and progress. After recording, review the saved run. Device permissions, connectivity and app versions affect which details are available.

07

Training after a race or time off

When a course ends or you take time off, choose what to do next. Relevant history and saved answers can help with setup, but check your current schedule, training preparation and recovery. Saving a new goal does not activate another course, and your earlier records stay in place.

08

Editing and sharing workouts

Use the editor to arrange warmup, work, recovery and cooldown blocks with time or distance targets. After a run, review its details or make a share card with your own photo. You still need to judge whether a workout suits your preparation.

Common questions

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. iPhone can guide the workout on its own. A paired Apple Watch lets you follow progress and use controls on your wrist. The data you can see depends on your devices and the permissions you allow.

Do I need a race goal or a VDOT value?

Not always. You can start with run-walk sessions or a free run. Some courses need pace guidance, relevant training experience and enough time to prepare.

Will saving a race replace my current course?

No. Save the goal, then arrange training separately. Your existing course and history stay in place. A new course still needs its own review of the workouts and preparation requirements.

Will poor sleep automatically change my workout?

No. Sleep and heart-rate information may prompt you to check how you feel, but RunCraft will not use it to calculate a replacement workout. Normal-looking data does not mean you are ready to train.

How do I continue after a course or time off?

Choose what you want to do next and check whether your saved running experience still reflects your situation. Before starting another course, review your available time, preparation and recovery.

Where does my data go?

Your devices process the data, and selected records can sync through your private iCloud database. You do not need a RunCraft account. There are no ads or third-party analytics. Diagnostic files go to support only if you choose to share them.
