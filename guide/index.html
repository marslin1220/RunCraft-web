Guides

Understand the plan. Make it your run.

A practical guide to choosing a course, following a session and planning what comes next.

01

Start with a direction

Choose regular running, a race goal or a free run. For a race, enter the date and exact distance, with an optional name. Save it first; arrange training separately when you are ready.

02

Bring the background that matters

Review recorded running and previously saved information where available, including their dates. Recent reduced running may not describe your usual training. Correct changed details and answer course-specific questions about experience, available days and time. Missing records do not automatically make you a beginner.

03

Check the course before using it

A goal alone does not establish preparation for a demanding programme. Review the sessions, schedule and relevant requirements. If your event, timeline or preparation does not fit a supported course, review the explanation in the app before choosing a different direction.

04

Use paces as guidance

VDOT connects a performance value to training pace guidance. Easy, Marathon, Threshold, Interval and Repetition work serve different purposes. An estimated race time is not a promised finish time, and a VDOT value alone does not establish experience with every workout.

05

Read observations with their dates

Sleep, HRV and resting heart rate depend on available measurements and comparison history. Missing or older readings cannot confirm today’s recovery. Observations can prompt caution or review; they do not automatically rewrite a workout, diagnose a condition or grant permission to train.

06

Follow and review a session

Use iPhone or a paired Apple Watch for guided workouts. Check the current step, target and progress while recording, then review the saved run and its available details. Device permissions, connectivity and supported app versions affect which information is available.

07

Plan again without losing the past

After a course, race or interruption, choose what comes next explicitly. Relevant recorded history and attributed saved inputs can help with the next setup, but current availability, preparation and recovery still need review. Saving a new goal does not automatically activate a new course.

08

Make room for your own workouts

Use the workout editor to arrange warmup, work, recovery and cooldown blocks with time or distance targets. Run details and photo-based share cards help you review and share a completed run. These tools do not determine whether a particular workout is appropriate for you.

Before you lace up.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Guided workouts can run directly on iPhone. A paired Apple Watch adds on-wrist guidance and controls. Data availability depends on your devices and permissions.

Do I need a race or a VDOT value?

Not for every path. You can start with run-walk training or a free run. Some courses need relevant experience, pace guidance and enough preparation time.

Does saving a race create or replace my course?

No. Save the goal first, then arrange training separately. Existing sessions and history are retained; the course has its own review and preparation requirements.

Will poor sleep automatically change my workout?

No. Recovery observations provide context and caution. They do not automatically establish a replacement workout or certify that you are ready to train.

What happens after a course or a break?

Review the next direction and relevant saved background. A new course needs its own review; old records do not automatically confirm current preparation or recovery.

What happens to my data?

RunCraft does not require a RunCraft account or use advertising or third-party analytics. Data is processed on your devices; selected records can sync through your private iCloud database. Optional support exports are shared only when you choose.
